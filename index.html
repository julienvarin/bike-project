The idea

Blue Lug is a Tokyo shop that made the everyday city bike into a craft object: a neutral quality-steel frame — often raw or a single clean colour — built with Japanese boutique parts (Nitto, MKS, Brooks, Paul) and given one or two playful colour pops through grips, cable housing and small brass details. The bike stays quiet; the accents do the talking. Your raw Trek is an ideal canvas for exactly that.

The parts, one by one

Green label = keep & refresh what you have. Amber label = the upgrade worth making. Prices are rough EU street prices for planning, not quotes.

Frame & finish

00

The foundation, and the part you don’t replace. A good True Temper steel frame, already stripped to bare metal — the look people pay framebuilders for. But raw steel rusts, so this is the one urgent job.

Keep · seal it soon

Two finishes fit the brief: clear powdercoat (durable, keeps the raw-steel look) or a single bold colour if you’d rather let the parts do the talking. Either way, add Frame Saver inside the tubes.

do: clear-coat or 1-colour powder + internal frame saver
cost: ≈ €60–120 at a coater · don’t leave bare over winter

Handlebar

01

Changes the whole character of the bike, and where Blue Lug’s DNA lives. The bar you sit behind is Nitto — the Japanese maker the shop is basically synonymous with. For an upright klunker-into-city feel, a wide swept-back riser is the move.

Upgrade · the personality choice
pick: Nitto Bosco B2500AA (swept riser)
alt: Nitto bullhorn (RB-018) for a leaner look
clamp: 25.4 mm · cost: ≈ €70–85

Stem

02

The stem clamps the bar to the fork. Yours is a quill — it slots down into the fork rather than clamping outside it — because the bike has an old threaded headset. So you need a quill stem, not a modern one. Handily, the answer is the same family as the bar.

Upgrade · must match the headset
pick: Nitto Technomic (tall polished quill)
clamp: 25.4 mm — matches the Bosco
verify: quill Ø = 22.2 mm (1″) on this era · cost: ≈ €55–70

Grips

03

The cheapest big decision. This is your first colour-pop opportunity — and it sets the tone for the whole bike. Warm and classic, or one loud accent?

Upgrade · decide the mood here
warm: cork or leather grips (match the saddle)
pop: bright lock-ons / ESI silicone in one colour
cost: ≈ €20–45

Saddle

04

You’re halfway there — a brown leather saddle is exactly right. If yours is comfy, keep it. The one upgrade people actually notice is a classic leather perch that breaks in to your shape.

Keep · or upgrade to the icon
pick: Brooks B17 in honey
note: ties the whole warm palette together
cost: ≈ €110–140

Tires

05

The single biggest visual transformation. Swapping knobbies for tan-wall or cream slicks reads “intentional build” from across the street. 26″ narrows the field, but two options nail it.

Upgrade · highest impact per euro
pick: Panaracer Pasela, amber wall (rando look)
fat: Schwalbe Fat Frank, cream (cruiser look)
verify: size marked 26 × 1.x on the casing · cost: ≈ €30–40 each

Pedals

06

Usually the ugliest thing on an old MTB. The fix is MKS — polished metal Japanese pedals, often with toe clips or leather straps. Silver metal underfoot instantly lifts the build.

Upgrade · small part, real signature
pick: MKS Sylvan Touring + toe clips
wide: MKS Allways / Lambda for flat-shoe comfort
cost: ≈ €55–90

Drivetrain

07

Yours has a triple crank and old shifters. The tidiest Blue Lug-style move is a 1× conversion: keep one front chainring, drop the front derailleur and left shifter, run the existing rear gears. It declutters the cockpit and looks deliberate.

Upgrade · simplify for the clean look
cheap: single ring + spacers on current crank
boutique: polished Sugino crankset
also: fresh chain + cassette · cost: ≈ €40–160

Brakes

08

Those are cantilevers — two arms on frame posts joined by a straddle cable. Period-correct and worth keeping. The cheap win is better pads; the dream-build swap is CNC’d brakes the shop reveres.

Keep · upgrade the pads (or go boutique)
cheap win: Kool-Stop salmon pads (≈ €25)
boutique: Paul Neo-Retro cantis + Paul/Dia-Compe levers
cost: pads €25 · Paul set ≈ €180–220

Finishing details

09

Everything above builds a tasteful bike. This is what makes it a Blue Lug bike. The shop’s signature is coloured cable housing — running the cables in a bright colour that repeats an accent elsewhere. Cheap, and the whole vibe.

Upgrade · where the shop’s look happens
housing: Jagwire / Nissen in your pop colour
bell: Crane “Suzu” brass bell
cage: Nitto or King silver bottle cage
cost: ≈ €70–110 all-in

Rack & fenders

10

Optional phase two, for the touring/rando end of the spectrum. A Nitto front rack with a small bag is almost a shop signature, and Honjo hammered alloy fenders make it look properly handbuilt.

Optional · save for last
rack: Nitto front rack (≈ €110–150)
fenders: Honjo hammered alloy (≈ €110–140)

Measure before you buy

Three numbers to confirm first

Old MTBs vary, and the wrong number means a returned part. Check these before ordering anything:

  • Seatpost diameter — read it off the post, likely 27.2 mm. Sets the saddle post + any dropper thoughts.
  • Headset / steerer — this era is usually 1″ threaded, which is why the stem is a quill. Confirms the Technomic fits.
  • Rear hub spacing — measure the rear dropout gap, often 135 mm here. Governs any wheel or cassette change.

Build order

Tap to tick off. Sequenced so the bike is rideable at the end of each phase — nothing here needs the next phase to work.

P1 Seal & roll (a weekend)

  • Clear-coat or powder the frame + Frame Saver the one urgent job
  • Fit tan/cream tires biggest visual change
  • Colour grips + coloured cable housing the Blue Lug pop
  • Kool-Stop salmon pads + fresh chain safety + shifting
  • Crane brass bell the jewel

P2 Cockpit & contact points

  • Nitto Bosco bar + Nitto Technomic quill stem
  • MKS pedals + toe clips
  • Brooks B17 honey (if upgrading the saddle)
  • 1× conversion — drop front mech + left shifter

P3 Boutique & touring (optional)

  • Paul Neo-Retro cantis + levers
  • Sugino crankset
  • Nitto front rack + bag
  • Honjo hammered fenders
