The idea

The approach: a neutral quality-steel frame — raw, chromed, or one clean colour — built up with well-chosen Japanese and boutique parts (Nitto, MKS, Brooks, Paul, Maxxis) and given one repeating colour pop. Here the frame and cockpit stay quiet in chrome and raw steel, and a single electric blue does the talking — echoed in the brake cables, the grips and small anodised bolts. Tokyo’s Blue Lug is one big influence, but this is your Trek, built your way.

Measure before you buy

Three numbers to confirm first

Old MTBs vary, and the wrong number means a returned part. Check these before ordering anything:

  • Seatpost diameter — read it off the post, likely 27.2 mm. Sets the C17’s seatpost.
  • Headset / steerer — this era is usually 1″ threaded, which is why the stem is a quill. Confirms the Technomic fits.
  • Rear hub spacing — measure the rear dropout gap, often 135 mm here. Governs any wheel or cassette change.

Build order

Tap to tick off. Sequenced so the bike is rideable at the end of each phase — nothing here needs the next phase to work.

P1 Seal & roll (a weekend)

  • Clear-coat or powder the frame + Frame Saver the one urgent job
  • Fit Maxxis urban slicks + tubes biggest visual change
  • Electric-blue grips + electric-blue brake cables the pop, in two of three spots
  • Kool-Stop salmon pads + fresh chain safety + shifting
  • Crane brass bell + blue anodised bolts the jewels

P2 Cockpit & contact points

  • Chrome riser bar + chrome Nitto Technomic quill stem
  • Polished mini V-brakes + short-pull levers
  • MKS pedals + toe clips
  • Brooks Cambium C17 (all-weather)
  • 1× conversion — drop front mech + left shifter

P3 Boutique & touring (optional)

  • Paul Mini Moto mini-V (the boutique brake)
  • Sugino crankset
  • Nitto front rack + bag
  • Honjo hammered fenders
